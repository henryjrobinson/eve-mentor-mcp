## The story to tell (all of this actually happened, most of it in one day)

- In the 80s/90s, my stepbrother and I spent ages designing our dream game: a space
  MMO with a real player-driven economy, real PvP, real consequences. Down to the
  details. We were just kids.
- Then CCP shipped it. EVE Online was *exactly* the game we had talked about. We were
  both stoked.
- He never got into it. I kept trying to sell him on it until he passed away in 2015 —
  and I couldn't really make the pitch, because the honest truth was too hard to admit:
  the game we dreamed up wasn't any fun to actually learn.
- My own record proves it. Character created August 2011. Lifetime stats: **0 kills,
  8 losses, across 15 years.** I logged in about once a year, played a month, hit a wall
  nobody explained (the Uedama gank chokepoint, a fit with three mismatched weapon
  systems, a station I suddenly couldn't dock at with all my stuff inside), and quit.
- So in June 2026 I built the mentor I needed, with Claude Code, in one day:
  **eve-mentor-mcp**, an open-source MCP server that connects Claude to live EVE data.
  16 tools: loss analysis, recursive skill planning, a career sorting hat, fit checking,
  ammo/damage matching, a jargon glossary, live PLEX economics, an asset finder.
- The first authenticated session is the heart of the post:
  - It **found my lost stuff** from the docking mystery — two asset-safety wraps that had
    waited years for me (the game never explains asset safety; my tool flags it).
  - It told me the exploration frigate I always wanted (the Astero) was **16,000
    skillpoints — about ten hours — away.**
  - It revealed past-me had quietly banked **17 million skillpoints and 702 million
    ISK** across all those failed attempts. I was never a failed newbie. I was a mid-tier
    veteran who couldn't find the door.
  - The career sorting hat looked at my answers and my skills and said: explorer. My
    skill sheet agreed — Astrometrics V was already trained. Past-me knew.
- Same day: open-sourced (MIT), site live at ruby-eve.com (named for Ruby Q, the
  character), her actual in-game portrait on the page.
- Hook for the ending: this is also, quietly, finishing a conversation that started in a
  kid's bedroom forty years ago.
