# DRAFT REMINDER: "We Designed EVE Online Before It Existed. I Still Can't Play It."

> Status: blurb only — write the full post later. Personal blog, not VL content.

## The story to tell

- In the 80s/90s, my stepbrother and I spent ages designing our dream game: a space
  MMO with a real player-driven economy, real PvP, real consequences. Down to the
  details. We were just kids.
- Then CCP shipped it. EVE Online was *exactly* the game we had talked about. We were
  both stoked.
- He never got into it. I kept trying to sell him on it until he passed away in 2015 —
  and I couldn't really make the pitch, because the honest truth was too hard to admit:
  the game we dreamed up wasn't any fun to actually learn.
- My own record proves it. Character created August 2011. Lifetime stats: **0 kills,
  8 losses, across 15 years.** I log in about once a year, play for a month, hit a wall
  nobody explains (a gank chokepoint, a fit with three mismatched weapon systems,
  a station I suddenly couldn't dock at with all my stuff inside), and quit.
- EVE's community calls it a learning cliff, not a curve. The data backs it: of 10,000
  new players, fewer than 500 remain at day 30.
- So I'm building the thing I needed: an open-source MCP server that turns Claude into
  an EVE mentor — it reads your real losses and explains why you died, tells you exactly
  what to train to fly the ship you want, and sorts you into a playstyle that fits your
  life. Built BY the guy who bounced off the game for two decades, FOR everyone like him.
- Hook for the ending: this is also, quietly, finishing a conversation that started in a
  kid's bedroom forty years ago.
